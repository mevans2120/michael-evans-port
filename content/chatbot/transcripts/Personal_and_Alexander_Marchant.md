# Alexander Marchant Case Study

I started working on Alexander Marchant about halfway through the design phase. The project was a little behind schedule.

Alexander Marchant sells luxurious home goods, including items like their marble bathtub, which is astoundingly beautiful and high-end.

Alexander Marchant's primary customers are interior designers working on million-dollar home renovations and remodels. Their target audience isn't necessarily buying directly through the website, but the site serves as a useful tool for tracking and visualizing the goods they'd like to purchase. The site offers a resource for interior designers, enables e-commerce sales where possible, and appeals to direct consumers as well.

We built the site on WordPress with a blog and an innovative, interesting design on the front end. Bravo powered the commerce system. When you navigate from the marketing side to the e-commerce side, there's a slight disconnect between the two platforms.

This project taught me how quickly we could build with WordPress in 2024. Even pre-AI, the available toolset allowed us to get features out the door remarkably fast. This was one of the few projects where we actually built more features than the client wanted. We ended up pairing some back because they didn't need all the blog features we'd developed. That was a learning experience for me—I don't think I'd ever been able to over-deliver to that degree, even with the small amount of time we had.

Alexander Marchant is a lovely client and easy to root for. They have showrooms in Austin and other parts of Texas, as well as in Oklahoma. Their e-commerce challenge stems from selling so many different product types. When you consider the goods an interior architect needs—like seeing all the different finishes a brass pull might have—there's enormous variety. Managing thousands of SKUs as a small shop is genuinely challenging.

That's one of the key areas we're working on going forward with Alexander Marchant.
