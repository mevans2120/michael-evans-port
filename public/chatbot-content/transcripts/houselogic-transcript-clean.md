House Logic was a project that came to Huge from the National Association of Realtors. It was a blue sky project—they were looking for a website for homebuyers. The National Association of Realtors is a collection of real estate agents, an association, not technically a for-profit enterprise. The goal was to create an excellent consumer product for the National Association of Realtors.

The design team, before I was working on the project, came up with this idea for House Logic. It was basically a set of tools for homeowners to track their home projects and potentially how much home value they were adding. This was intended to slot in next to content sites like This Old House and others.

It was one of those interesting sites where there were a couple of things I learned.

One, it was a Django project, Django CMS. This was quite a while ago, before single page apps. Having a very talented development team and really following the software development process—this was my second big custom website project—I learned that following this process and communicating well and keeping your feature list tight meant that you could ship on time. Which we did. I think we were one of the first development projects at Huge that shipped on time with little complication.

The other thing we learned was what the site ended up being. House Logic ended up being a content site, not a tool-based site. Having these tools on the web just wasn't something that consumers were really going to do yet. I think a lot of these tools now exist, but at the time, the preferences weren't there. It was desktop only, before mobile phones, so it was a little ahead of its time as far as a tool-based site.

We definitely spent, as far as engineering goes, way more time engineering the tools than we did the content site. But I think the positive from this was the pivot to a content site whose focus was still on ways to improve home value with remodeling. Focusing on things like bathrooms and kitchens adding way more home value than, say, a hot tub, however nice those may be.

Those were the two big learnings. Following a process—this was more of a waterfall, half-agile type process—can pay dividends. And then the other thing is that it's okay to pivot. It should be encouraged. Even if you've sunk a bunch of time into pursuing one strategy, pivoting and ignoring the sunk cost is really the benefit.

And then the last thing is, I think as I was writing this case study, I discovered that House Logic got put to pasture. Rest in peace.
